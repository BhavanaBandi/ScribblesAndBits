---
title: "Maybe I Am Growing After All"
month: "September"
year: 2026
---

Dear beautiful people,

I think we have been lied to about growing up.

Somewhere along the way, we were made to believe that becoming an adult would be this very obvious thing. You turn a certain age, get a job, pay your bills, make important decisions, and suddenly, congratulations! You are a Proper Adult.

But I don't think it works like that.

I think adulthood happens quietly.

Sometimes, it looks like getting your first salary. Sometimes, it looks like making a difficult decision without asking ten people what they think first. Sometimes, it looks like realising that a friendship has run its course and choosing to let it go without turning it into a war.

And sometimes, apparently, it looks like getting a nail cutter.

I have a terrible habit of biting my nails. I've done it for years. It's one of those tiny habits that you don't even think about anymore — you just find yourself doing it.

But today, I noticed my nails getting long, and instead of biting them, I went and got a nail cutter.

And I cut them.

That's it.

Nothing particularly life-changing happened. No one applauded. No inspirational music started playing in the background.

But I was ridiculously proud of myself.

Because I realised that maybe growth isn't always about becoming an entirely new person.

Sometimes it's simply noticing yourself doing something and choosing differently.

And that got me thinking about how strangely personal growth is.

There isn't really a universal definition of what it means to "grow up."

For some people, growth is becoming more independent. For someone else, it might be learning to ask for help.

For some, it might be learning to speak up.

For others, it might be learning when to stay quiet.

It might be leaving a relationship. Or choosing to stay and work through it.

It might be becoming more ambitious.

Or finally learning that you don't need to be productive every second of the day.

Maybe growth is learning how to say no.

Maybe it's learning how to say sorry.

Maybe it's realising that not every disagreement needs to become a battle you have to win.

And I think one of the strangest parts of growing up is what it does to the way we see other people.

When we're younger, everything can feel so black and white.

Someone hurt you? They're a bad person.

Someone disappointed you? They don't care about you.

Someone changed? They don't love you anymore.

But somewhere along the way, you start understanding that people are complicated.

You realise that everyone is carrying things you cannot see.

You realise that someone can love you deeply and still hurt you.

You can be disappointed in someone and still understand why they did what they did.

You can outgrow a friendship without deciding that the friendship was meaningless.

And perhaps most importantly, you begin to give people a little more grace.

Not because you suddenly think everything is acceptable.

But because you understand that you are also a work in progress.

You have made mistakes that you wish people had forgiven you for.

You have said things you didn't mean.

You have reacted badly.

You have misunderstood people.

You have been selfish sometimes, impatient sometimes, and probably a little impossible sometimes.

And yet, you grew.

So maybe adulthood is partly learning to extend that same grace to the people around you.

Not endlessly. Not at the cost of yourself.

Just enough to remember that nobody is finished becoming who they are.

And I think the same applies to us.

We are constantly looking for some grand indication that we've grown. We want the career, the relationship, the confidence, the independence — something tangible that proves we're moving forward.

But maybe we should pay more attention to the tiny things.

The way you handle an argument differently now.

The fact that you don't chase people who don't choose you anymore.

The way you can sit alone without feeling lonely.

The way you apologise without immediately trying to justify yourself.

The way you can celebrate someone else's success without comparing it to your own.

The way you finally choose to cut your nails instead of biting them.

These things seem insignificant until you realise that they are evidence.

Evidence that you are not the same person you were a year ago.

And yes, being an adult comes with responsibilities.

There are bills and deadlines and careers and difficult conversations. There are decisions that nobody can make for you anymore. There are moments when you realise that being independent doesn't mean knowing what to do, it means being willing to figure it out.

And perhaps that's the part of adulthood that nobody really prepares you for.

You are still figuring it out.

Everyone is.

The person who looks like they have their entire life together probably has something they're quietly struggling with. The person who seems incredibly confident is probably still questioning themselves somewhere. The person you think is "ahead" of you might be measuring their life against someone else too.

We're all just walking around with different versions of adulthood.

Different responsibilities.

Different timelines.

Different definitions of success.

Different things we're still learning.

So maybe we should stop measuring growth by how far ahead we are and start measuring it by how different we are from who we used to be.

Because adulthood isn't a finish line.

It's a stepping stone.

And there will be many more after this one.

There will be new jobs, new friendships, new cities, new mistakes, new versions of ourselves.

And I hope we learn to notice them.

I hope we celebrate the tiny victories.

I hope we forgive ourselves for the things we are still learning.

I hope we give the people around us a little more grace.

And I hope that every once in a while, we stop and realise:

Oh.

I handled that differently this time.

Maybe I am growing after all.

Even if nobody else notices.

Even if it looks ridiculously small.

It still counts.

Yours with a smile,
Bhavana
